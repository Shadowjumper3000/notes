## Definition

> If choice between two heuristics, use the larger one.
> If neither overestimates one is probably underestimating

- Problem-solving methods using approximations or rules of thumb
- Focus on speed and practicality over guaranteed optimality

## Key Properties

- Not guaranteed to find the best solution
- Aim for "good enough" solutions within reasonable time
- Useful in large or complex search spaces

## Advantages
- Faster than exhaustive methods
- Often simpler to implement
- Effective when exact methods are infeasible

## Disadvantages

- No guarantee of optimal solutions
- May fail on certain problem instances
- Performance depends on problem-specific heuristics

## Common Types

1. **Greedy algorithms** – choose locally optimal step at each stage
2. **Local search** – iteratively improve a candidate solution
3. **Metaheuristics** – higher-level strategies guiding heuristics, e.g.:
    - Genetic algorithms
    - Simulated annealing
    - Tabu search
    - Ant colony optimization

## Applications

- Route planning
- Scheduling
- Optimization
- Machine learning (feature selection, hyperparameter tuning)
- Game AI
